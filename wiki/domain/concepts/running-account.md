## Definition

A running account is a balance that grows as a customer buys now and pays later, usually in one payment. Each purchase is added to the balance instead of being paid at the counter. Paying the balance, often once a month, is called settling it, and the balance then starts again from zero. It rests on the shop trusting the customer to pay. It is also called a tab, a khata or a house account.

## Usage

A shop keeps a running account for a customer it trusts, or for a business that buys for its people. Each purchase is written against the account. The shop or the customer can check the balance at any time, and settling pays all of it or part of it.

> [!warning]
> A running account is not the same as a credit balance in the customer's favor. Here the balance is what the customer owes the shop.

## Examples

- A tea stall keeps a running account for a regular customer. The customer pays the whole balance at the end of the month, and the account starts again from zero.
- An office pantry supplier keeps one running account for a whole company. Every employee's snacks are added to it, and the company pays the total at the end of the month.
- A shopkeeper keeps a khata notebook with one page for each regular customer, and writes each purchase on that customer's page.

## Non-examples

- A cash sale. The customer pays at the counter, so no balance builds up.
- An allowance. Money is set aside in advance and spent down, so the amount shrinks, where a running account grows.
- A credit card. A bank lends the money and the shop is paid at once, so the shop holds no balance.

None.
